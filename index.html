How to Use This Simulator

Prescription Values

SPH (Sphere) is the main corrective power. Negative values (−) are for nearsightedness; positive (+) for farsightedness. The stronger the number, the thicker the lens and the more optical distortion to manage.

CYL (Cylinder) & Axis correct astigmatism — an uneven curvature of the eye. The axis (0–180°) tells the lab which direction to orient the cylinder grind. High CYL values create the "oblique distortion" pattern you see as the heatmap twists.

Decentration (D_mm) is the distance from the optical centre to the farthest lens edge. The dashed circle shows it; beyond it is dimmed. Larger values mean thicker minus edges and more of the blurry periphery inside the frame.

Reading the Heatmap

The map is calculated, not drawn: every point traces a real ray from the eye's centre of rotation through the designed lens surfaces (Snell's law + Coddington's equations), and the colour is the predicted eye-chart acuity there for a 4.5 mm pupil, looking at whatever distance that zone is made for.

Cyan/Blue — about 20/20–20/25. Effortless.
Green — about 20/40, the driving standard. The white line marks this boundary.
Yellow — about 20/70. Readable but soft.
Orange — about 20/130. Turn your head instead.
Red — 20/200 or worse. Hatched grey = so far off-axis the eye can't look through it.

The dark grid is a square grid as it really appears through the lens. Lines that bend or lean are the distortion patients feel as "swim" when they move their head.

Lens Types Compared

Progressive

A digital free-form design (the Kirkland Signature HD Digital Progressive class). Distance to infinity at the top, reading at the bottom. Minkwitz's law sets the corridor: unwanted cylinder grows sideways twice as fast as the add grows down, so stronger adds give narrower corridors (about 6 mm at +2.00).

HD Multi-Functional

An office lens: a smaller power change over a longer corridor, so the middle is about 3× wider than a progressive. The top keeps extra plus, so vision is sharp to about 6 ft and soft beyond. Ideal for computer work, crafts and reading — not driving.

Lens Cross-Section

The silhouette in the canvas corner shows the actual profile of your lens (not just thickness). A plus lens (farsighted) is thickest in the center, like a magnifying glass. A minus lens (nearsighted) is thinnest in the center with thick edges — which is why high-minus lenses are often called "coke-bottle" glasses. The shape also explains why plus lenses make eyes look bigger, and minus lenses make them look smaller.

Screen Calibration

For accurate physical sizing, click Auto-Detect first. Then look at the reference bar drawn in the lower-left corner of the lens canvas — it represents 50mm. Physically measure it with a ruler, type that measurement into the box, and click Apply. All lens dimensions will now reflect true physical scale on your screen.
