How to Use This Simulator

Prescription Values

SPH (Sphere) is the main corrective power. Negative values (−) are for nearsightedness; positive (+) for farsightedness. The stronger the number, the thicker the lens and the more optical distortion to manage.

CYL (Cylinder) & Axis correct astigmatism — an uneven curvature of the eye. The axis (0–180°) tells the lab which direction to orient the cylinder grind. High CYL values create the "oblique distortion" pattern you see as the heatmap twists.

Age sets how much focusing your eyes still have (about two-thirds of 18.5 − 0.3 × age dioptres). It decides how the Computer and Reading tasks look through the top of the lens.

Frame & Fit: the lens shape, eye size (A), lens height (B) and bridge (DBL) are the numbers printed inside the temple; the map is edged to that shape. Your mono PD sets where your pupil sits in it. The farther the frame's centre is from your pupil (decentration), the thicker the outer edge and the more off-axis lens you look through.

Reading the Lens Map

The map is calculated, not drawn: every point traces a real ray from the eye's centre of rotation through the designed lens surfaces (Snell's law + Coddington's equations), for a 4.5 mm pupil. It is seen from your side and edged to your frame, so the nose is on the left for the right eye.

All-in-one colours each point by whichever is worst there: blur for the chosen task, unwanted cylinder (0.5 D ≈ Good, 1 D ≈ Soft) or swim (6% ≈ Good, 12% ≈ Soft). Tap any point to see all three. The other chips show one quantity at a time.

Great — nothing to notice (about 20/25 or better).
Good — minor (20/40, the driving standard).
Noticeable — soft or swims (about 20/50–20/70).
Strong — turn your head instead (20/80–20/200).
Severe — avoid. Hatched grey = so far off-axis the eye can't look through it.

The dark grid is a square grid as it really appears through the lens. Lines that bend or lean are the distortion patients feel as "swim" when they move their head.

Lens Types Compared

Progressive

A digital free-form design (the Kirkland Signature HD Digital Progressive class). Distance to infinity at the top, reading at the bottom. Minkwitz's law sets the corridor: unwanted cylinder grows sideways twice as fast as the add grows down, so stronger adds give narrower corridors (about 6 mm at +2.00).

HD Multi-Functional

An office lens: a smaller power change over a longer corridor, so the middle is about 3× wider than a progressive. The top keeps extra plus, so vision is sharp to about 6 ft and soft beyond. Ideal for computer work, crafts and reading — not driving.

Lens Cross-Section

The Lens Profile under the map shows the actual profile of your lens (not just thickness). A plus lens (farsighted) is thickest in the center, like a magnifying glass. A minus lens (nearsighted) is thinnest in the center with thick edges — which is why high-minus lenses are often called "coke-bottle" glasses. The shape also explains why plus lenses make eyes look bigger, and minus lenses make them look smaller.

Screen Calibration

For accurate physical sizing, click Auto-Detect first. Then measure the white bar under the Lens Profile with a ruler (it is labelled with the length it should be), type that measurement into the box, and click Apply. With Actual size on, the lens map is then drawn at true size, so you can hold your frame or lens up to the screen.
