How to Use This Simulator

Prescription Values

SPH (Sphere) is the main corrective power. Negative values (−) are for nearsightedness; positive (+) for farsightedness. The stronger the number, the thicker the lens and the more optical distortion to manage.

CYL (Cylinder) & Axis correct astigmatism — an uneven curvature of the eye. The axis (0–180°) tells the lab which direction to orient the cylinder grind. High CYL values create the "oblique distortion" pattern you see as the heatmap twists.

Decentration is how far the optical center is shifted from the lens blank center (in mm). Larger values increase peripheral distortion.

Reading the Heatmap

Cyan/Blue — Clear, undistorted vision. In progressive/HD modes, blue indicates far-distance capability.
Green — Excellent clarity. The comfort zone of the lens.
Yellow — Mild distortion. Most people adapt well to this region.
Orange — Moderate distortion or limited distance range. May cause swim or blur.
Red — Strong distortion. Most patients avoid looking through these zones after adaptation.

The grid lines show how objects appear to warp through the lens. Straight lines that curve represent the "swim" effect patients notice when moving their head.

Lens Types Compared

Progressive

Distance at top, reading at bottom, with a narrow transition corridor. The top zone is blue — true distance vision to infinity. Wider prescriptions and high ADD powers make the clear corridor narrower. Best for general all-day wear.

HD Multi-Functional

Camber™ technology maximizes the intermediate and near zones. The corridor is 2–3× wider than progressive, but the top zone is deliberately amber/orange — max distance is ~13ft. Ideal for computer work, crafts, and reading.

Lens Cross-Section

The silhouette in the canvas corner shows the actual profile of your lens (not just thickness). A plus lens (farsighted) is thickest in the center, like a magnifying glass. A minus lens (nearsighted) is thinnest in the center with thick edges — which is why high-minus lenses are often called "coke-bottle" glasses. The shape also explains why plus lenses make eyes look bigger, and minus lenses make them look smaller.

Screen Calibration

For accurate physical sizing, click Auto-Detect first. Then look at the reference bar drawn in the lower-left corner of the lens canvas — it represents 50mm. Physically measure it with a ruler, type that measurement into the box, and click Apply. All lens dimensions will now reflect true physical scale on your screen.
